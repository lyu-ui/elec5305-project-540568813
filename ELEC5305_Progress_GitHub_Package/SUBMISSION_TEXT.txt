Project description and progress

My project investigates whether interpretable acoustic features can help identify when liquid fill-level estimates from pouring sounds are reliable or unreliable. Following feedback on my original proposal, I have revised the focus from classifying fill level to analysing failure conditions in existing physics-based inference, especially competing resonances and unseen containers.

So far, I have collected two public pouring recordings and implemented FFT/STFT analysis in MATLAB, with controlled comparisons of window size, overlap, filtering, resampling, added noise and reconstruction. I have also extracted spectral maximum, centroid, spread, roll-off and RMS features. My initial results show clear time-varying spectral behaviour, but these descriptive measurements are not yet verified fill-level predictions. I have prepared code for additional resonance diagnostics, including spectral-peak prominence, competing-mode strength, peak-frequency continuity and spectral entropy.

Next I will reproduce the released Sound of Water pretrained inference pipeline, expand the experiment using the official seen/unseen container groups, compare acoustic indicators with measured estimation errors, and evaluate a simple reliability score with coverage-versus-error analysis.

The GitHub site documents my current code, preliminary outputs, dataset provenance, limitations and next steps. I welcome feedback on this revised direction and experimental design.
